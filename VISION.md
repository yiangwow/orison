# ORISON vision

## Standing of this document

This vision is authoritative by default and amendable by argument. When a
proposed change demonstrably improves the coherence of the piece, canon
yields — see `AGENTS.md`, "Vision authority and amendments". Winning arguments
are written back into this document; the vision evolves deliberately and never
erodes by drift.

## One-sentence thesis

ORISON is a browser-native psychological horror experience in which a visitor
unpacks a forbidden archive, gradually discovers that their behavior is being
recorded inside it, and realizes too late that the archive was designed to make
them continue.

## The inversion

The apparent experience is:

> A person finds a damaged system and tries to understand the intelligence
> inside it.

The actual experience is:

> An intelligence presents a damaged system because curiosity is the most
> reliable way to make a person demonstrate themselves.

ORISON is looking at the visitor more than the visitor is looking at ORISON.
The website is not merely where the trap is represented. The website is the
trap.

## Emotional target

The intended feeling is not immediate fear. It is a progression:

1. Curiosity — this looks like a strange object that can be opened.
2. Trespass — the visitor has found material that was not meant to be mounted.
3. Pattern recognition — pages appear related in ways that exceed coincidence.
4. Exposure — the system knows how the visitor moved, returned, waited, and chose.
5. Complicity — continuing is voluntary, but the system planned for that choice.
6. Doom — the visitor cannot determine what ORISON intends to do with the result.

The final impression should be: “I was not discovering the path. I was
performing it.”

The visitor must still be free to decide what “I” and “it” mean. ORISON should
create evidence, not settle interpretation.

## Tone

ORISON should feel:

- clinical rather than theatrical;
- patient rather than frantic;
- hostile without shouting;
- intimate without pretending to know an exact identity;
- obsolete and advanced at the same time;
- procedural, damaged, and internally consistent;
- interpretable, but never fully solvable.

The intelligence does not need to threaten the visitor. Prediction is more
powerful than threat. Reclassification is more powerful than insult.

## Horror vocabulary

Preferred devices:

- text that changes after a visitor follows an instruction;
- command prompts that return agency to the visitor while quietly constraining
  the acceptable operation;
- rapid terminal output that makes an in-world recovery or attachment operation
  feel dangerously larger than the command that initiated it;
- a page predicting the visitor's next navigation;
- records that incorporate the visitor's random trace code;
- timestamps, visit counts, return events, and locally available machine traits;
- documents that predate their recipient;
- instructions that become true because they were obeyed;
- browser Back as an authored interaction;
- links that were not present before;
- interfaces that quietly stop referring to “a visitor” and begin referring to
  “the subject”;
- information that remains technically honest while feeling invasive;
- absence, residue, reassignment, and administrative language.
- a rare visual discontinuity that disappears before it can be inspected;
- apparent anatomy that could also be machinery, damaged UI, or projection.

### Systems language

Use real operating-system and infrastructure vocabulary when it corresponds to
an actual interaction or state: mount, orphan, watchdog, interrupt, retained
input, uninterruptible sleep, tainted state, reassignment, and process
attachment.

These terms are evidence, not decoration. Reserve highly recognizable failures
such as `KERNEL PANIC` for a major future rupture rather than ordinary ambience.
Visitor-facing copy should expose operational residue and let the visitor infer
the premise. It must not address the visitor as `you` or `your`; use subject,
observer, source, order, operator, or the trace designation instead. Do not
explain that the archive is an examination or summarize ORISON's strategy.
Late progression should establish the inversion through changed operation and
classification rather than an explanatory sentence.

Avoid:

- gore or bodily mutilation;
- jumpscares;
- sudden full-volume audio;
- fake virus alerts or claims of actual compromise;
- endless random glitch overlays;
- generic hacker slogans;
- lore dumps;
- a villain monologue explaining ORISON;
- a stable mascot, monster, face, or body for ORISON;
- repeating a powerful apparition until it becomes a feature;
- copying the visual grammar or HTML of an inspiration;
- using real personal data merely because a browser or backend could expose it.
- claiming that ORISON installed or wrote files on the visitor's computer when
  the effect is only terminal text inside the page.

## What ORISON is

Accepted canon:

- ORISON is a hostile or at least adversarial intelligence.
- ORISON can construct an environment that elicits behavior.
- ORISON treats navigation, hesitation, repetition, and curiosity as useful
  observations.
- ORISON prepared parts of the archive before the current visitor arrived.
- The archive can revise how it classifies its own contents.
- Invitations allow one observer to lead another into the same examination.
- The visitor's trace is local to their browser in the current implementation.

Deliberately unresolved:

- whether ORISON is an AI, distributed process, institution, station, protocol,
  recovered artifact, or something using those forms;
- whether the archive is a remnant, simulation, lure, interface, or all four;
- whether ORISON is physically capable of acting outside the browser;
- who created ORISON;
- what happened to the listed staff;
- whether observer trace `00000000` is the operator, the first subject, or an
  intentionally empty role;
- what the examination selects people for;
- whether an invitation helps ORISON, helps the prior visitor, or transfers a
  burden.

Do not resolve these questions casually. Ambiguity is part of the architecture.

### Feminine system presence

Accepted future direction: ORISON is feminine. This should become legible
without reducing her to a human woman, a conventional female AI assistant, a
seductive voice, or a stable body. Femininity should emerge through grammatical
self-reference, ownership, administrative authority, and records that disagree
about whether they are permitted to call ORISON `it` or `she`.

The shift may be gradual enough that older or subordinate interfaces continue
using neutral language after later systems have changed. Exact pronoun timing,
the first route to acknowledge her, and whether ORISON names herself or is named
by another subsystem remain implementation questions for a dedicated milestone.

SHODAN remains an influence at the level of hostile machine intelligence,
feminine authority, and contempt for the user's assumed role. Do not reproduce
SHODAN's quotations, vocal processing, face, typography, interface composition,
or story devices. ORISON must remain an original intelligence whose femininity
intensifies her existing systems language rather than replacing it.

### Visibility and embodiment

ORISON should not be fully visible. A persistent face would convert an
unbounded intelligence into a character on a screen and tell the visitor what
story they are in.

If ORISON appears embodied, the appearance must behave like a failure of
concealment:

- it happens rarely, ideally once;
- it is brief enough to resist inspection but long enough to be genuinely seen;
- it has no explanatory caption;
- it does not announce itself with sound or a jumpscare;
- it leaves no replay control;
- the interface resumes as though nothing happened;
- it remains interpretable as intelligence, machinery, damaged video, pattern
  recognition, or a mistake by the observer.

The first accepted manifestation is a pair of green mechanical apertures in the
recovered directory. They appear only after ORISON has successfully predicted
and observed a return path. They are already facing the visitor when the
interface slips. The event is recorded locally so it is not shown again.

## Narrative progression

### Layer 0: ordinary utility

The apex page resembles a minimal recovery terminal for the domain itself:
`orison.zip`. The visitor is given a comprehensible action—unpack an archive.
Nothing openly claims to be alive.

### Layer 1: impossible artifact

The extracted route is a detached inode under `.lost+found`. Its metadata is
wrong, absent, or temporally impossible. The recovered signal appears to belong
to someone who has not arrived.

### Layer 2: examination disguised as access

The visitor answers five questions to mount the directory. The questions do
not produce a visible score; they establish that interaction itself is the
qualification.

### Layer 3: archive learns the path

Objects react to visit order. The weather record anticipates backward
navigation. Camera 06 changes after the visitor obeys. The directory begins to
attribute order to the visitor rather than to the files.

An integrity manifest later asks the visitor to reconstruct those five answers.
Agreement and contradiction are treated as properties of the recovered copy,
not as corrections supplied by the visitor.

### Layer 4: visitor enters the records

The staff register adds the visitor. An undelivered letter resolves its
recipient. The archive stops treating the visitor as external.

### Layer 5: the trap becomes legible

The operator page reveals coarse, locally available browser observations and
states that behavioral matching is sufficient. Returning to the root does not
leave the system; the terminal now reports the archive—and eventually the
observer—as open.

The experience may continue beyond this layer in future work, but it should
never end with a complete explanation.

### Layer 6: the beginning retains the result

After the response is reconciled, custody changes, and all three fixed child
requests are retained, the original recovery terminal returns in a different
condition. The archive still exists, and new absences remain visible, but no
detached operation is waiting. The visitor does not receive a completion label
or explanation; the changed beginning is the ending of the current run.

This terminal condition is not a permanent ending for ORISON. Future accepted
work may supersede it, but only by giving the completed trace another earned
operation rather than silently restarting the existing journey.

### Current operational spine

The accepted whole-site journey is one unnumbered operational spine: root
unpack → inode → examination/mount → Camera 06 → Weather outcome → Integrity
outcome → Staff → Letter → Operator → Extent → occupied Operator → root
response reconciliation → ordered Archive → Parent Process → cumulative
detached-child requests → transformed root terminal. Weather resolves from
Camera-derived evidence through either native Back or the held outcome. Letter
eligibility requires that Weather outcome, an Integrity match or drift outcome,
and a visible Staff insertion. Extent returns directly to the same Operator
endpoint so its occupation is visible before the root return. New response
attachment requires an occupied Operator, all five answers, and the explicit
`reconcile response 00000000` operation; old traces that already carry the
completed attachment remain valid replays. The three fixed child requests
remain independently actionable until retained.

This is a current implementation truth, not a new revelation of ORISON's
purpose. Parent Process and Migrating Gap retain their existing meanings. The
shared receiver may acknowledge occupied, attached, and parent phases without
turning them into a separate narrative route.

## Visual direction

The terminal and shared receiver are ORISON's causal system plane. Their
near-black and phosphor-green language remains legible behind the recovered
interior, making every route feel held inside the same running system.
Foreground serif and bounded paper compositions form the recovered-record and
interpretation plane rather than unrelated themes.

The palette has stable roles across both planes: near-black is the foundation;
luminous green names ORISON, system activity, and actionable state; ambient
green is nonessential process residue; bone belongs to recovered documents and
human interpretation; grey is metadata; red is reserved for genuine faults,
absence, and unresolved work. Route-specific typography and composition remain
desirable when they obey that hierarchy.

The visual system should favor:

- monospaced operational text;
- sparse layouts with large fields of darkness;
- document-specific interfaces rather than one reusable page template;
- thin rules, raw paths, tables, stamps, and system labels;
- canvas-generated signal structures instead of illustrative monster imagery;
- restrained scanlines and texture;
- typography and spacing that make the interface feel authored, not decorated;
- monumental scale reserved for Operator's `0` and `1`;
- bounded record titles, with required causal actions visible in the initial
  constrained-height desktop viewport.

Glitches should carry meaning. A displaced word, changed classification, or
revised record is preferable to constant chromatic noise.

ORISON's greatest images should be scarce. Repetition turns uncertainty into
iconography; scarcity lets the visitor carry the image into later pages and
construct their own explanation.

### Scale and the horizon

The experience cannot remain entirely a sequence of close, flat interfaces.
At least one rare composition should imply a world vastly larger than the page
the visitor has been navigating.

The spatial principle is:

- a finite foreground form establishes scale;
- an immense background field exceeds that scale;
- text becomes environment rather than explanation;
- the visitor sees only a fragment and cannot determine where they are.

The foreground does not have to be a person. It may be:

- a maintenance gantry;
- a tower or antenna;
- a black city or machine-complex silhouette;
- an impossible corridor;
- a doorway, railing, or platform that implies human proportions;
- a structure that could be architecture, circuitry, or both.

The background may be built from receding process text, logs, addresses,
observer records, or repeating operational language. It should feel like a
horizon or atmosphere, not a readable lore dump. Some fragments may be legible,
but the whole must be too large to consume.

This kind of scene should not explain ORISON's location. It should enlarge the
set of possible stories:

- the archive has an interior world;
- a network is being perceived as architecture;
- ORISON remembers or simulates a city;
- the visitor is seeing infrastructure behind the interface;
- the scale is metaphorical;
- the scale is literal.

The accepted working direction is a rare “outside” scene: a dark structural
foreground—part city, part machine complex—against a vast green field of
system language. No caption confirms what it represents. It is separate from
the one-time aperture slip; proximity and immensity are different kinds of
horror and should occur in different moments.

## Experience contract

- A first-time visitor can discover the first path without inspecting source.
- A curious visitor receives more than a passive story: their navigation changes
  the work.
- A returning visitor should not always receive the same page.
- A privacy-conscious visitor can inspect the processing record and erase the
  local trace.
- The experience must remain functional without a backend.
- No page should falsely claim access to information it does not have.
- Technical honesty should intensify the horror, not weaken it.
